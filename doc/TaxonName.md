Species: the basic evolutionary unit. A set of populations that form a distinct lineage and are diagnosable by heritable traits. Competing but compatible lenses:

* Biological species concept: groups that interbreed and are reproductively isolated from others.
* Phylogenetic species concept: the smallest monophyletic cluster on a tree, uniquely diagnosable.
* Morphological species concept: distinguished by stable structural characters.
  For diatoms and many microbes, phylogenetic + morphological evidence dominate, because reproductive tests are impractical.

Genus: the next rank up. A cluster of closely related species that together form a monophyletic group and share key traits. Each genus has a type species that anchors the name.

Specific epithet: the second word in a species name. Latinized, lower-case. It has no standing by itself; only **Genus + epithet** constitutes the species name.

How they relate:

* Binomial name = **Genus epithet**. Example: *Achnanthes affinis*.

  * Genus = *Achnanthes* (capitalized, italicized).
  * Specific epithet = *affinis* (lower-case, italicized).
* The epithet often describes a feature, place, or person (adjective, noun in genitive, or noun in apposition). If adjectival, it agrees with the grammatical gender of the genus. When a species moves to a different genus, the epithet usually follows, sometimes with an ending change for agreement.
* Authorship and date may follow (e.g., *Achnanthes affinis* Grunow), and parentheses around the author indicate a change of genus since the original description.

Rules and scope:

* Plants, algae, and fungi (including diatoms) follow the ICN; animals follow the ICZN. Both codes enforce priority, typification, and rank hierarchy.
* Typification: a physical type (specimen, illustration, or type culture) fixes the application of a name. A genus is fixed by its type species; a species by its type specimen.
* Infraspecific ranks add a third term: *subsp.*, *var.*, *f.* (trinomials). These are below species and above population.
* Provisional markers in field data:

  * **sp.** = unidentified species in a known genus (*Achnanthes sp.*).
  * **cf.** ≈ “compare to”; tentative ID.
  * **aff.** ≈ “related to”; near but not that species.
  * These are not formal names.

Quick glossary:

* Species (singular = plural “species”): basic unit; a lineage with diagnostic traits.
* Genus (plural “genera”): a group of related species; has a type species.
* Specific epithet: second word; only meaningful with its genus.
* Binomen: the two-word species name.
* Monophyly: all descendants of a common ancestor; required ideal for genera.

Practical heuristic:

* Think tree: tips = species; small branches = genera. The label at a tip is the binomen; the branch grouping several tips is the genus name reused across its species.

Infraspecific ranks = taxonomic levels **below species**. They add a third term after the binomen.

Core ranks and concrete examples:

* **Subspecies** (*subsp.*)

  * Animals (ICZN: only subspecies is formal):

    * Dog: *Canis lupus subsp. familiaris* (domestic dog).
    * Tiger: *Panthera tigris subsp. tigris* vs *P. t. subsp. altaica*.
    * Gorilla: *Gorilla gorilla subsp. gorilla* (western lowland).
  * Plants, algae, fungi (ICN):

    * Maize: *Zea mays subsp. mays* (cultivated) vs *Zea mays subsp. parviglumis* (teosinte).

* **Variety** (*var.*) \[ICN only]

  * Cabbage complex: *Brassica oleracea var. capitata* (cabbage), *var. italica* (broccoli), *var. gemmifera* (Brussels sprouts).
  * Diatoms (classic usage): *Cocconeis placentula var. euglypta*; *Fragilaria capucina var. rumpens*.

* **Form** (*f.*) \[ICN only]

  * Minor, often color or morphology:

    * Maple: *Acer saccharum f. nigrum* (often treated as subsp. in some works).
    * Horticultural “albino” forms occur across many taxa (e.g., white-flowered forms).

* **Special forms in pathogens**

  * **forma specialis** (*f. sp.*) \[ICN, host specificity]:

    * *Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici* (tomato wilt).
    * *Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici* (wheat stem rust).
  * **Pathovar/biovar/serovar** \[bacteria; nomenclatural infraspecifics outside ICN/ICZN]:

    * *Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica* **serovar** Typhimurium.
    * *Vibrio cholerae* **biovar** El Tor.
    * *Pseudomonas syringae* **pv.** tomato.

Formatting rules:

* Write as *Genus species rank-abbrev. infraspecific-epithet*.
  Example: *Quercus robur subsp. robur*; *Brassica oleracea var. italica*.
* Genus, species, and infraspecific epithets are italicized; rank words (**subsp.**, **var.**, **f.**, **f. sp.**) are not.

How they relate:

* **Species** is the base unit.
* **Subspecies** groups geographically or genetically distinct populations within a species.
* **Variety** and **form** capture finer, usually morphological differences in plants, algae, and fungi.
* Animal taxonomy formally recognizes only **subspecies** below species; plants/fungi/algae use **subsp.**, **var.**, **f.**

Heuristic:

* If you see three terms, you are at an infraspecific rank.
  Example tokens: `subsp.`, `var.`, `f.`, `f. sp.`, `serovar`, `biovar`, `pv.`
